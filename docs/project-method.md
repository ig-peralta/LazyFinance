# Capstone methodology (metodología)

LazyFinance uses **iterative and incremental development with agile practices, without a formal Scrum framework**. This is the approach described in the [Phase 1 project definition](fase_1/guia_estudiante.docx) and [technical report](fase_1/informe.docx), and carried forward in the [Phase 2 progress guide](fase_2/guia_avance.docx). The [product requirements](product-requirements.md) describe what the product must achieve; this document explains how we plan, build, check, and record progress.

## Why this approach fits

Financial answers depend on decisions about balances, transfers, budgets, income, ownership, and data freshness. A large integration-first build could hide mistakes in those meanings until late in the semester. Small, demonstrable increments let us test those decisions against repeatable CLP demo scenarios, revise the model, and then add the UI, identity, bank adapters, and agent without conflating a working interface with validated financial results. The team can reprioritize work when a scenario exposes a missing capability while retaining acceptance criteria and a record of the decision.

The Phase 1 guide proposed starting with Fintoc. The Phase 2 guide and the current product requirements record the change to **core validation before Fintoc**. The Stage A gate (LAZ-18) is documented in the [Stage A closure record](planning/snapshots/stage-a-closure.md); subsequent work proceeds in parallel under the Stage B milestone. This change in sequence is part of the project history, not a claim that the initial Fintoc integration is already delivered.

## Planning and execution

1. **Define the outcome.** The [product vision](product-requirements.md#1-product-vision) and [user journeys](user-journeys.md) provide the problem, intended result, and observable scenarios. The team tracks acceptance and verification criteria on its Linear issues; the reviewer-facing [backlog snapshot](planning/snapshots/backlog-2026-11-02.md) reproduces their titles, status, and workstream outcomes.
2. **Choose a small increment.** Within the team's Linear project, identify dependencies, priority, ownership, and the next demonstrable result. **Each stage milestone is one sprint** (Sprint A, B, or C), and **the issues assigned to that milestone are its Sprint Backlog**. The [backlog and stage evidence guide](planning/backlog.md) and dated snapshot make the plan readable without tracker access. The team does not claim a formal Scrum framework or uniform sprint durations.
3. **Implement and validate.** Work is reviewed in Git and checked against the issue's acceptance criteria, relevant automated tests, and user-scoped financial behavior. The [backlog snapshot](planning/snapshots/backlog-2026-11-02.md) summarizes each issue's individual **Done when** condition; the [sprint product definitions of done](planning/sprint-definitions-of-done.md) and [product Definition of Done](planning/product-definition-of-done.md) apply to broader closure decisions. Completing one issue does not close a sprint or release the product.
4. **Review and adjust.** Demonstrate the result against representative scenarios, record what passed or remains open on the issue, and change the next increment if necessary. Decisions affecting scope or financial meaning belong in the relevant product/technical document and issue. **At stage closure**, capture the milestone's issues and gate outcome in a dated repository [record](planning/snapshots/stage-a-closure.md); Linear continues to hold the live backlog. An interim [all-issue snapshot](planning/snapshots/backlog-2026-11-02.md) is available for reviewers while Stage B is still active.

The original Phase 1 plan listed Ignacio's initial focus as banking connectivity and the text agent, Alejandra's as financial logic and the dashboard, and Álvaro's as MCP and voice. The Phase 2 guide explains that work is shared rather than restricted to those areas. A task's current Linear assignee records its owner when one has been assigned; every team member participates in planning, validation, and documentation.

## Sprint Backlogs and stage snapshots

Work has been tracked by issues and stage milestones. When a stage is completed, retrieve its milestone issues from Linear and preserve a **dated closure record** in the repo, including issue IDs and completion times, the gate decision, Git baseline, retrospective analysis, and the date the record was assembled. [Stage A's closure record](planning/snapshots/stage-a-closure.md) centers on its 2026-10-21 GO decision and last issue closure; its provenance explains that it was assembled afterward and does not represent a start-of-stage commitment. The [all-issue snapshot](planning/snapshots/backlog-2026-11-02.md) gives reviewers a self-contained view of active Stage B planning without mirroring live issue changes continuously.

The Stage A, B, and C milestones are **Sprint A, Sprint B, and Sprint C** in our adapted planning vocabulary. Each milestone's issues are its Sprint Backlog; there are **no separate Linear cycles or smaller sprints**. The [Stage A closure worklist](planning/snapshots/stage-a-closure.md) shows what belonged to Sprint A when reviewed after closure, but the original selection was not captured before work began and it cannot prove a historical sprint-planning meeting. For future sprints, record the goal and selected issues in the milestone **at planning time** using the [stage-sprint record fields](planning/stage-sprint-record-template.md), then include the outcomes in the repository's closure record. A closing export cannot infer original selections from current issue fields.

**Stage C is planned after Stage B, not pre-populated now.** Once the Stage B gate and retrospective clarify what was delivered and remains open, the team will create and select Stage C issues in Linear, then track their priorities and ownership under the Stage C milestone. The current Stage C description expresses product direction, not a committed issue set.

The [Stage A documentary retrospective](planning/stage-a-retrospective.md) analyzes issues, commits, and the gate review after the fact. It is not a record of a team retrospective meeting.
